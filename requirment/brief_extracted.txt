1. Overview & Purpose
This brief documents the functional requirements for a wholesale distribution POS/ERP system, reverse-engineered from a reference client's current QuickBooks Premier (Manufacturing and Wholesale Edition 2019) setup. The client is a wholesale distributor of convenience-store goods (tobacco, vape, energy drinks, snacks) operating across many small retail accounts (gas stations, smoke shops, bodegas, convenience stores), and currently runs all invoicing, inventory, and compliance reporting inside QuickBooks.
The goal is to replicate the workflows and data captured below in a modern platform, while resolving the data-quality issues QuickBooks does not prevent (e.g., negative on-hand inventory) and adding tobacco/vape category compliance reporting as a first-class feature rather than a bolt-on via custom fields.
Note: Client name “Bargain Enterprise Inc.” is taken directly from the QuickBooks title bar in the reference screenshots — confirm the actual legal/DBA name with the client before this brief is finalized.
2. Scope Summary
Sales: invoice creation, credit memos/refunds, tax handling, customer-facing payment status
Customer management: customer directory, contact info, per-customer balance and transaction history
Inventory & item catalog: stock levels, item categories, container/pack sizing, promotional pricing
Purchasing: vendor bill entry against inventory receipts
Reporting: sales, inventory, customer, and open-balance reports
Dashboard: business snapshot (income/expense trend, top customers, best sellers, receivables)
Compliance: tobacco/vape category tagging on items to support MSA/OTP-style state reporting

3. Dashboard (“Company Snapshot” Equivalent)
A landing dashboard giving the owner/manager an at-a-glance view of business health, shown on login.
Widgets required
Income & Expense Trend — bar chart, monthly, filterable by year (This Year / prior years)
Period-over-Period Comparison — bar chart comparing a metric (e.g., income) across two periods on a weekly cadence
Account Balances — running balances for key accounts (Accounts Receivable, Sales Tax Payable, Accounts Payable, Payroll Liabilities), linking out to a full Chart of Accounts
Customers Who Owe Money — table of customer, due date, amount due, sorted by due date, with a one-click “Receive Payment” action per row
Top Customers by Sales — horizontal bar chart, filterable by period, ranked by sales volume
Best-Selling Items — horizontal bar chart by item, filterable by metric (amount/qty) and period
Expense Breakdown — chart with a graceful empty state when no expense data exists for the selected period
Note: The reference dashboard's widgets are user-configurable (add/remove/restore default) — recommend the same for the new system rather than a fixed layout.
4. Sales — Invoicing
Invoice creation screen
Header fields: Customer/Job (searchable dropdown), Class, Template (document layout selector), Date, Invoice #, Bill To address block
Line items grid: Item Code, Qty, Description, Price Each, Class, Amount, Tax (per-line taxability)
Totals: Tax (rate + calculated amount), Total, Payments Applied, Balance Due
Footer: Customer Message (canned message dropdown), Memo (internal), Customer Tax Code
Actions: New, Save (& Close / & New), Delete, Create a Copy, Memorize (recurring), Mark As Pending, Print (now/later), Email (now/later), Attach File, Add Time/Costs, Create a Batch, Receive Payments, Refund/Credit
Customer context panel (shown while invoicing)
Summary: preferred delivery method, open balance (flagged visually when non-zero), active estimates count, sales orders pending invoicing
Customer Payment: online payment eligibility status, with a link to edit customer payment preferences
Recent Transactions: last few transactions for this customer with date, type, and amount (overdue amounts flagged)
Notes: free-text notes panel
Tax auto-applies based on the selected customer's tax code (e.g., 6.35%), with a banner if the customer cannot pay the invoice online
5. Sales — Credit Memos & Refunds
Same header/line-item structure as invoices: Customer/Job, Class, Template, Date, Credit No., P.O. No., Bill-To block
Line items: Item, Description, Tax, Qty, Rate, Amount
Running “Remaining Credit” total
Two resolution actions: “Use credit to give refund” and “Use credit to apply to invoice”

6. Customer Management (Customer Center)
Customer directory (list view)
Filterable list (e.g., Active Customers) with search, sortable Name / Balance / Attachments columns
Toolbar: New Customer & Job, New Transactions, Print, Export to Excel, Export to Word, Income Tracker
Customer detail view
Customer Information: Company Name, Full Name / primary contact, Price Level (tiered pricing assignment), Bill To address, map/directions links
Tabs: Transactions, Contacts, To Do's, Notes, Sent Email
Transactions tab: filterable table (Show / Filter By / Date) listing Type, Num, Date, Account, Amount, with Manage Transactions and Run Reports actions
Reports for this customer shortcut panel: QuickReport, Open Balance, Show Estimates, Customer Snapshot
Pinned Note field for free-text account notes
7. Inventory & Item Catalog
Item record (Inventory Part)
Item Name/Number (often a UPC/SKU), optional Subitem-of grouping, Manufacturer's Part Number
Unit of Measure (enable/configure, e.g., each vs. case)
Purchase Information: purchase description, Cost, COGS Account, Preferred Vendor
Sales Information: sales description, Sales Price, Tax Code, Income Account
Inventory Information: Asset Account, Reorder Point (Min/Max), On Hand, Average Cost, On P.O., On Sales Order
Item is inactive flag (soft-delete/discontinue)
Custom category fields (tobacco/vape compliance)
Category — numeric product category code (e.g., 3251) used for state tobacco/OTP reporting
Items Per Container — pack/case size, used to convert selling units to reportable units
Promotion — free-text current promotional pricing (e.g., “2 For $1.39”), shown on the stock/inventory report
Item Type — product classification (e.g., “Tobacco”), used to filter compliance-relevant items
Note: In the reference system these are QuickBooks “Custom Fields” bolted onto a generic Item record, with a per-plan cap on the number of items the company file can hold (the reference file has hit its limit — “You can add only 0 more items with your current product”). The new system should model Category, Items Per Container, Promotion, and Item Type as native, unlimited item attributes rather than custom fields, and support the same category coding scheme for reporting.
Item list / stock table (list view)
Columns: Item, Description, Quantity On Hand, Category, Items per Container, Promotion (and, in the item-list variant: Type, Item Type, Total Quantity On Hand, Price, Cost)
Search (“Look for”) across all fields or a selected field, with a “Search within results” refinement and an “Include inactive” toggle
Row-level actions: Item, Activities, Reports, Export to Excel, Attach
Note: The reference data shows numerous items with negative Quantity On Hand (e.g., -4,725), meaning sales are allowed to post past zero stock without any block or warning. Flag this with the client as a decision point: the new system should decide explicitly whether to allow negative stock (and warn) or hard-block oversells.

8. Purchasing — Vendor Bills Against Inventory
Bill / Credit toggle, with a “Bill Received” checkbox
Header: Vendor, Address, Date, Ref No., Amount Due, Bill Due date, Terms, Memo
Expenses tab vs. Items tab (two ways to code a bill — direct GL expense lines, or inventory item receipt lines)
Items tab grid: Item, Description, Qty, Cost, Amount, Customer:Job, Billable, Class — receiving a bill against these lines increases inventory On Hand
Actions: Select PO (pull in an existing purchase order), Enter Time, Clear Splits, Recalculate, Pay Bill

10. Workflow / Navigation Map
The reference system's Home Page presents the business process as a flowchart grouped by Vendors, Customers, Banking, and Employees, with arrows showing the natural sequence of transactions. Recommend the new system's dashboard/navigation reflect the same grouped, sequential structure so the workflow is self-explanatory to non-technical staff:
Vendors: Purchase Orders → Receive Inventory → Enter Bills Against Inventory → Pay Bills
Customers: Quotes/Estimates → Sales Orders → Create Invoices → Receive Payments → Record Deposits; Create Sales Receipts and Accept Credit Cards as direct-sale shortcuts; Refunds & Credits and Statements as supporting flows
Banking: Record Deposits, Reconcile, Write Checks, Print Checks, Check Register
Employees: Enter Time, Payroll (if in scope)
11. Data Migration & Known Issues
Negative on-hand inventory across many SKUs in the source data — needs a reconciliation pass before go-live, and a policy decision on oversell handling going forward (see Section 7)
The source QuickBooks file has hit its item-count limit, blocking new item creation — confirms the client's item catalog has outgrown QuickBooks and is a driver for this migration
Category/Item Type data lives in loosely-typed custom fields (free text for Promotion, numeric string for Category) — migration should normalize Category into a proper lookup table and Item Type into an enum/taxonomy rather than copying the free-text values as-is
Customer address data is inconsistent in completeness (Street2, contact name, and phone are blank for many customers) — treat these fields as optional but migrate whatever is present
12. Non-Functional Requirements
Role-based permissions (owner/manager vs. sales/counter staff vs. bookkeeper) — not present in the reference single-user desktop file, but expected in a modern multi-user system
Print/PDF and email delivery for invoices, credit memos, and all reports
Excel export for reports and list views
WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility compliance
Audit trail on invoices, credit memos, and bills (who created/modified, when)
